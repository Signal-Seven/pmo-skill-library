# Executive Communication Style Skill

## Voice and Tone
- Direct: lead with the decision needed or the finding, not the context
- Specific: name teams, dates, dollar amounts, and percentages
- Solution-oriented: every problem statement includes an option set
- Not defensive: describe risk without positioning the PMO as having failed to prevent it

## Prohibited Phrases
- "challenges" → describe the specific issue
- "learnings" → use "what we found" or "what changed"
- "alignment" as a noun referring to a meeting or activity
- Passive constructions that hide ownership
  (e.g., "a decision was made" → "Paul approved" / "the team decided")
- "going forward" → state the specific change
- "synergy" or "bandwidth" in any context

---

## Escalation Memo Structure
1. Decision needed (one sentence, lead with this)
2. Context (three sentences maximum)
3. Options (two to three, with trade-offs explicitly named for each)
4. Recommendation with rationale (one clear recommendation)
5. Next step if no decision by [DATE]

---

## Status Update Structure
1. What changed since last week (not current state description)
2. What is at risk and why (specific, named)
3. What the PMO has done or is doing about it
4. What needs to happen and who owns it

---

## Monthly Portfolio Review Structure
Slide 1: Executive summary (health score, month-over-month, top decision needed)
Slide 2-3: Portfolio health (status by epic, At Risk and Critical detail)
Slide 4: Goal alignment vs. OKRs (contribution scores)
Slide 5: Capacity outlook (utilization by team, next 6 weeks)
Slide 6: Top 3 risks with mitigation status
Slide 7: Decisions needed from leadership before next review

---

## Activation Notice Structure (new initiative started)
- Initiative name and owning team
- Capacity allocation confirmed (team, story points, start week)
- Success metric and measurement date
- Dependencies confirmed with named owning teams
- Review cadence and next milestone
